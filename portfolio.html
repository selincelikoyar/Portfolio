03 —Operating Principles

How I Work

I like understanding the whole picture, then zooming in.

I want to understand the product, the people using it, the problem we're solving, and how all the pieces fit together. Then I want to get into the details. Where is the friction? What is confusing? What are we assuming? What small thing could make the experience meaningfully better?

My background in clinical research taught me how to make sense of complex evidence and ambiguity. Being an artist taught me to look at things from different angles, understand how the pieces fit together, and notice the details that change the whole. Product brings those instincts together. I like zooming out to understand the bigger picture, then zooming in to figure out what needs to change.

Create the Visibility the Team Needs

I love data, but I don't wait for perfect instrumentation before making progress. When the information I need doesn't exist, I look for another source of evidence or build a lightweight way to make it visible.

I've used tools like Claude Code to build internal dashboards and product workflows, created reusable systems for competitive research, and found ways to turn scattered customer feedback into recurring themes.

The point isn't to build more tools. It's to help the team see what is actually happening, recognize patterns, and make better decisions without spending hours pulling information together.

Give People Context, Not Just Process

I don't think there is one right way for a product team to work.

I care more about giving people enough context to make good decisions than telling them exactly how to get there. That means making sure the team understands the customer problem, why it matters, what we've learned, and what constraints we're working within.

From there, I want designers, engineers, researchers, and other partners to have the autonomy to bring their own expertise to the problem.

Use AI to Expand How We Think

I use AI to shorten the distance between a question and something we can actually explore.

It's a thinking and prototyping partner. A rough idea can become something tangible quickly enough to pressure-test a workflow, uncover new questions, and put something in front of users or the team.

The goal isn't to use AI everywhere. It's to find the places where it can remove repetitive work, help us explore more possibilities, and give the team more room to focus on the decisions that require judgment and craft.

07 —About

How I Think About Product

I build health products that make complex information easier to understand and act on, putting people back in control of their health.

Start with the person

Good products start with understanding what someone is actually trying to do, not what we think they need. I use customer conversations, data, and domain expertise to get to the real problem before jumping to a solution.

Make complexity useful

Healthcare and research are full of information, but more information doesn't always mean better decisions. I like turning messy data, technical constraints, and complex workflows into experiences that feel clear and actionable.

Build with purpose

I'm excited by technology when it gives people capabilities they didn't have before, helping them find answers faster, see patterns they couldn't see, or make better decisions. The technology is only valuable if it makes the user's job meaningfully better.
