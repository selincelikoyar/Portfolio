03 —Operating Principles

How I Work

I like working at the intersection of evidence, experimentation, and team context. When the information needed to make a product decision does not exist, I am comfortable finding another way to uncover it — or building a lightweight system that makes it visible.

Create the Visibility the Team Needs

I love data, but I do not wait for perfect instrumentation before making progress. When the visibility I need does not exist, I look for another source of evidence or build a lightweight way to capture it.

I have used tools such as Claude Code to create lightweight internal dashboards and product workflows when the visibility I needed did not already exist — alongside reusable workflows for competitive market analysis and systems for consolidating customer feedback into recurring themes. The goal is not to add more tooling. It is to help the team identify patterns, make better-informed decisions, and spend less time assembling information manually.

Give People Context, Not Just Process

I do not believe there is one universally correct way for a product team to work. My role is to understand how designers, engineers, researchers, and other partners work best, then create the conditions that allow them to make strong decisions with appropriate autonomy.

That means providing clear customer problems, strategic context, decision boundaries, and access to relevant evidence rather than attempting to control every step of the process.

Use AI to Strengthen Judgment — Not Replace It

When we considered how our design process should evolve in an AI-assisted development environment, I did not begin by asking which additional AI tools we should introduce. I focused first on what designers needed to do their best work.

We found that better context was more valuable than simply adding better tools. We began involving design earlier by sharing customer pain points, workflows, personas, research, and competitive context; putting rough concepts in front of customers sooner; and continuously circulating feedback so the entire team developed a stronger understanding of the people we were building for. See how this approach shaped the Medeloop Analytics redesign.

I use data creatively to improve decisions, but I also pay close attention to the environment in which those decisions are made. Creating better visibility while helping people do their best work is how I like to operate.

07 —About

How I Think About Product

I build products that make complex things easier to understand, act on, and ultimately put back in the hands of the people using them.

Start with the person

Good products start with understanding what someone is actually trying to do, not what we think they need. I use customer conversations, data, and domain expertise to get to the real problem before jumping to a solution.

Make complexity useful

Healthcare and research are full of information, but more information doesn't always mean better decisions. I like turning messy data, technical constraints, and complex workflows into experiences that feel clear and actionable.

Build with purpose

I'm excited by technology when it gives people capabilities they didn't have before, helping them find answers faster, see patterns they couldn't see, or make better decisions. The technology is only valuable if it makes the user's job meaningfully better.
